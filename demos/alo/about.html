The association

Founded in 1944 as the volunteer arm of the Louisville Orchestra. About 350 members, a 35-member board, and one job.

Since 1944

Eight decades of one idea

The Association of the Louisville Orchestra was founded in 1944, the same year the orchestra started the competition we still run every fall. The idea then is the idea now: an orchestra needs an audience and an audience needs a reason to show up, and a volunteer association can build both in a way an institution cannot.

Since 1944 the association has given the Louisville Orchestra more than $2.9 million. Most of that has come through A la Carte, the events programme we started in 1987 and still run at about thirty evenings a year.

We are roughly 350 members and a 35-member board. Nobody here is paid. The parties are hosted in members' own homes and underwritten by members' own money, which is the reason the full price of a ticket can go to the orchestra.

The mission, in three sentences

  1. Provide funding and resources to support the Louisville Orchestra.
  2. Create opportunities for youth to excel in performing and appreciating symphonic music.
  3. Broaden the audience and support of the Louisville Orchestra throughout the region.

What changed

The $2.9 million figure is stated here once, and every other page reads it from the same record. On the current site the home and about pages credit it to the association “since its inception in 1944” while the A la Carte page credits the same $2.9 million to a programme “founded in 1987” — one number with two origin stories, on a site asking people for $750. Which reading is correct is the first open question in the PRD; this build assumes the association's total since 1944, raised mostly through A la Carte since 1987.

Raising funds

Where the money comes from

Three programmes, all of them volunteer-run, all of them sold or collected through this site.

A la Carte experiences

Roughly thirty parties, dinners, outings, and day trips a year, running since 1987. Board members and friends of the association sponsor and underwrite every one of them, which is why the full ticket price goes to the orchestra rather than to the cost of the evening.

Dine & Donate

Restaurants across Louisville host an ALO night and give a share of the evening's proceeds to the association. You have dinner where you were going to have dinner; the orchestra gets a cut.

Remember with Music

Honorarium and remembrance gifts, directed to music education for young people in the region. The family of the person honored receives a note; the gift goes to work on a child's music education.

Encouraging youth

Where the money goes

The association funds the orchestra directly, and it funds the next generation of the people who play in it.

Young Artist Competition

A hallmark event since 1944. Musicians in grades 5–12 from within 125 miles of Louisville compete each fall for a cash award and the chance to perform as guest soloist with the Louisville Orchestra.

MakingMusic concerts

The association provides lunches for Louisville Orchestra musicians at these concerts for fourth and fifth graders — the concert many children in this region attend before they attend any other.

The instrument playground

Members volunteer at pre-concert activities at family concerts, where children get their hands on instruments the association has refurbished or bought outright.

Broadening audiences

Members are the audience, and they bring the audience

Members attend Louisville Orchestra concerts, bring guests who have never been to one, and advocate for the orchestra across the city and the region. It is the least formal part of the mission and the part that compounds.
