Ministry philosophy

In his own words.

I think the church was supposed to be a hospital, and most of us have built a waiting room instead. A waiting room is where you sit quietly, look presentable, and hope nobody asks how you are really doing. A hospital is where you go when something is actually wrong. Everything I believe about leading a church comes back to that.

People do not need a church that is impressed with them. They need one that will still be there on the fourth try.

On preaching: the text decides what I say, not the news cycle. But a sermon that is technically correct and useless on Monday has not done its job. My aim every week is that somebody drives home knowing one true thing about God and one thing to do about it.

On leadership: I build teams, not a following. I have planted a church from nothing and I have been one of several executives at a thirty-thousand-person church, and the second one taught me more about staying out of the way. Staff should be able to tell me I am wrong in a meeting without paying for it later. Elders should have real authority, not the kind you thank them for at Christmas.

On growth: Mosaic grew fast and I am glad it did, but attendance is a by-product, not a scoreboard. What I want to know about a church is who is still there after they have seen how it works from the inside.

On the job itself: go to the hospital, do the funeral, answer the text message. Most of pastoring happens where nobody claps. A candidate who only wants the stage is not applying to be a pastor.

Fit

What he is looking for.

  • An elder board with real authority and the nerve to use it.
  • A congregation that would rather hear something true than something comfortable.
  • A staff that can disagree with him in a meeting and not pay for it later.
  • A church that wants to be a hospital and knows it is not one yet.
  • A town where Lindsay and the kids can belong as people, not as staff.

If that is your church, or the church you are trying to become, the conversation is worth having.
